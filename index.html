Visualization literacy is defined as the ability to understand graphical displays of data - something that has become a focal point within the visualization community for several years. The skill is often discussed in the context of assessments as they pertain to an adult's ability to consume graphical displays of information, but little is known to the visualization community about the full pathway from childhood education to adult applications of the skill. Despite this, there exists a plethora of education standards defined within the U.S. state-level governments that dictate how students are expected to learn this important skill. To date and to the best of our knowledge, no study has attempted to connect state-defined STEM education standards with pre-existing frameworks within visualization literacy, representing a clear gap in connecting current theory with real-world application. In this paper, we gather and collect 4925 standards from 20 states within the United States and conduct thematic analysis on those standards to contextualize existing frameworks in light of actual practices across the U.S. nation. In doing so, we contribute a corpus of all 50 states' STEM policy standard documents (as of January 2026), an annotated dataset of visualization literacy education standards, a framework of skills and their respective skill levels for visualization literacy, and a policy analysis methodology.
Poster Paper Citation: Julia Weppler, Mackenzie Michael Creamer, Michelle A. Borkin, "Visualization Literacy in United States Classrooms: A Qualitative Analysis of State Standards for K-12 Education Policy", IEEE VIS, November 2026.
Full Paper Citation: Mackenzie Michael Creamer, Julia Weppler, Michael Correll, Michelle A. Borkin, "Visualization Literacy in United States Classrooms: A Qualitative Analysis of State Standards for K-12 Education Policy," Unpublished.
